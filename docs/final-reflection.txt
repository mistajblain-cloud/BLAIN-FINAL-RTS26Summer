Final Project Reflection
  As this project, and semester, comes to an end I’d like to take some time to reflect on my 
personal growth and some of the things I found difficult or could have done differently and 
possibly better. Given my limited coding ability, this course has been one of the most challenging 
and rewarding classes that I’ve taken in UCF’s electrical engineering program. Coming into the course, 
I had some experience writing embedded C code, but I had very little understanding of what it takes to 
build reliable, deterministic software. Through the series of apps implemented on the ESP32-S3 using 
FreeRTOS and the ESP-IDF framework, I developed a deeper understanding of real-time operating systems 
and how they are used in safety-critical embedded applications.
  One of the most valuable lessons I learned was that writing code that simply works is very different 
from writing code that works predictably and reliably. Throughout the course, I learned to analyze task 
scheduling, priorities, preemption, interrupt handling, synchronization primitives, and worst-case execution 
time instead of focusing solely on functionality. Measuring execution time, calculating processor utilization, 
and verifying system timing gave me a much greater appreciation for real-time software design.
  The capstone project was particularly meaningful because it required integrating multiple periodic tasks, a 
button ISR, and collecting and logging performance data, as well as demonstrating real-time system behavior. 
Implementing tamper detection, integrity monitoring, CRC audit logging, and computational workloads forced me 
to consider task priorities, timing, and processor utilization. Rather than simply adding features, I had to 
justify the design with evidence such as heartbeat counters, worst-case execution time measurements, and 
scheduling analysis. This project reinforced the importance of designing systems that are both functional and dependable.
  Two things that I would have liked to have done differently are implementing more of what was learned on a physical board 
and having written tasks that were truer to their function, instead of just being simulated work. I think that I can accomplish 
this down the road if I work on my foundational programming skills and continue to utilize what I have learned in this class. 
I already have a physical ESP32 board and do plan to continue down this road.
  Overall, this course significantly improved both my programming ability and my understanding of embedded systems engineering. 
I now have practical experience designing, implementing, measuring, and validating real-time software. The knowledge gained this 
summer will be directly applicable to future coursework, especially senior design, in my personal projects, as well as in my career 
in electrical engineering.
